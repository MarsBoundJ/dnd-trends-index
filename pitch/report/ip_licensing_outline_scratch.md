## Phil's framing constraints (acknowledged at the top)

1. **Don't build the report from a single use case.** This document showcases the
   full range of what Trusight does for IP & Licensing — UB Matrix scoring,
   Buyers-Remorse Landmine detection, Reverse-Funnel Acquisition, and any new
   capabilities we surface from the three AI inputs.
2. **No straw-men.** Spy × Family is too easy a target — it isn't on WoTC's
   radar (slice-of-life family comedy, off-brand for D&D). The "Avoid" case
   study has to be apples-to-apples: an IP a licensing exec would *seriously*
   consider, where Trusight's data tells them no. See the Case Study IP
   Selection section below for candidates.
3. **Adheres to the comprehensive-report style guide.** Forum anonymization,
   OGL-scrub, technical-jargon scrub, calibrated-honesty patterns, loglines
   ~1 per page, no real-name WoTC executives.

## Strongest ideas from each AI input (ranked by strength)

### From ChatGPT — strongest framing
- **★ Reframe the UB Matrix dimensions as RISKS.** Fit → Translation Risk;
  Reception → Community Risk; Acquisition → Demand Risk; Commercial → Revenue

  scoring IPs; we're de-risking million-dollar bets.

  players (D&D vocabulary in non-D&D spaces, homebrew tied to IP, AO3 crossover
  density, "build/DM/campaign" mentions). This is a genuinely new core metric.
- **★ Convergence Threshold** — methodological argument: single-source signals
  lie, convergent ones don't. An IP becomes high-confidence when ≥3 independent
  ecosystems agree across different user behaviors (talk, write, build, buy).
- "Monday Morning Licensing Dashboard" — operational framing.

### From Gemini — financial heft + one killer new capability

  to "WoTC's design team can compress development cycle by ~40%." This is a
  genuinely new capability framing that bridges Licensing → Game Design.
- **Licensing Arbitrage** as the conceptual frame — finding undervalued IPs vs.
  overvalued IPs is the core financial story.
- **Negotiation-table examples** — "your audience has a 0.10 tabletop conversion
  intent; we will only offer a standard royalty." Concrete value at the deal

### From Perplexity — structural completeness

## Plan A — "The Decision Engine"

Frame: Trusight is the deal-by-deal de-risking tool. Each licensing decision
gets run through a structured 4-Risk evaluation. The killer new capability
is the "Free R&D" Homebrew Arbitrage that bridges Licensing → Design.

### Section-by-section
1. **Cover** — *"The $10M Mistake vs. the $50M Sleeper: How Trusight Rewrites
   IP Licensing Decisions"*. Anchored on a head-to-head case-study teaser.
2. **The Licensing Problem (Their Pain).** Three failure modes of IP licensing:
   the obvious trap (high awareness, weak D&D conversion), the silent failure
   (no discourse ≠ no risk), the missed sleeper (already converting, invisible
   to agencies). Position WoTC's current workflow as optimized for #1 and
   blind to #2 and #3.
3. **The 4-Risk Decision Framework.** Reframe the UB Matrix as risk-management.
   Translation Risk / Community Risk / Demand Risk / Revenue Risk — what kills
   deals on each axis, what Trusight signal de-risks each. This is the killer
   centerpiece.
4. **Convergence Threshold (the methodology argument).** Why ≥3-channel
   agreement is the threshold. Single-source signals lie; convergent ones
   don't. This is our methodological moat.
5. **Case Studies (4 IPs, apples-to-apples).** See IP Selection section below.
   Each case study: chart + UB scores + narrative + verdict.
6. **The "Free R&D" Homebrew Arbitrage.** New killer capability. Measure
   mechanical labor the community has already done for free. Bridges Licensing
   to Design — same data, two different teams' decisions. Concrete examples
   with adds-counts and homebrew artifact totals.
7. **Pre-Conversion Index.** New metric. % of fanbase already behaving like
   D&D players. Concrete calculation methodology + ranked candidates.
8. **Methodology, confidence & calibration.** "Reads against ourselves":
   strongest finding, weakest finding (still valuable), per-finding confidence
   grades, calibration plan (back-test against past D&D crossovers, re-run
   quarterly).
9. **Economic Impact / What This Proves.** Concrete value levers: avoid one
   bad deal saves $X; identify one sleeper generates $Y; reduce evaluation
   time from 6-10 weeks to 30 minutes. Closes on the "Trusight transforms IP
   Licensing from reactive vibes-based to proactive arbitrage" line.

### Tone notes
- Risk-management language throughout, not "data analytics" language.
- Financial framing (CAC, ROI, arbitrage, capital allocation) where appropriate.
- The "Free R&D" section can lean a bit more playful — the implication of
  "the community has been doing your design team's job for free" is a fun beat.
- Methodology section has the standard "reads against ourselves" honesty.

### Best for
Selling to Eugene's team (or any IP & Licensing leadership) as a tool to put
*on every individual deal*. The strongest pitch for "we should adopt this for
our next licensing review."

## Plan B — "The Pipeline Intelligence System"

Frame: Trusight is ongoing portfolio management — a system that runs every
quarter, surfaces the IPs about to enter peak monetization, and feeds licensing

### Section-by-section

2. **The Licensing Problem.** Same as Plan A but framed around pipeline

   monitoring. Reactive, not proactive.

   Acquisition / Commercial), with the optional risk-reframing as a presentation

## Case Study IP Selection — the apples-to-apples question

This is the hardest single question in the report. The case studies are where
Trusight's value gets demonstrated; if they don't land, the report doesn't land.

### The four archetypes we need

Per Perplexity's frame (which I think is right):
- **The Counter-Intuitive Greenlight (sleeper that wins)** — niche-looking IP
  with strong data signals.
- **The Counter-Intuitive Avoid (mainstream IP that wouldn't translate)** —
  this is where your straw-man concern bites hardest.
- **The Force-Fit Risk** — strong reception, weak fit. Brand halo > game-fit.
- **The Sleeper Find** — already-converting indie IP, near-zero CAC.

### Candidate IP set (with apples-to-apples in mind)

**Greenlight:** **Hollow Knight** — locked in. 50 DDB items, top item Vessel
species 269 adds, all signals positive. Our canonical greenlight case.

**Force-Fit Risk:** **Stranger Things** — locked in. We have data
(forum_presence_score 0.68, AO3 ao3_work_count 81). Plus there's the real-world
hook: WoTC has an existing Stranger Things crossover. Brand halo with weak
mechanical fit is the perfect "force-fit" demonstration.

**Sleeper:** **Dungeon Crawler Carl** — strong candidate. LitRPG audiobook
series, niche audience, but the reverse-funnel signal is high. Or **Berserk**
(we have data: 25 DDB items, sufficient_all_three).

**Avoid (the apples-to-apples question):** This is where I want your input.
The Demon Slayer / Walking Dead / Spy × Family choices all have problems:
- *Spy × Family*: your straw-man concern.
- *Walking Dead*: zombies are off-brand for D&D — also a bit straw-mannish,
  though it's the canonical real-world example.
- *Demon Slayer*: our data actually shows Demon Slayer as positive on homebrew
  (55 DDB items, 718-add Blood Hunter subclass). It would be the wrong "avoid"
  case.

**Three candidate replacements for the Avoid case:**

A. **The Witcher** — adult fantasy, monsters, RPG roots, popular Netflix show.
   Looks like a slam-dunk on the surface. The "no" case: CDPR has its own
   tabletop Witcher RPG, fans use that, AO3 D&D-crossover signal probably weak.
   The insight Trusight surfaces: when an IP has its own RPG ecosystem, D&D
   conversion CAC is *higher*, not lower. This is a sophisticated finding that
   no Hollywood-pipeline list would catch. **Apples-to-apples with Hollow Knight
   in the sense that a licensing exec would seriously consider both.**

B. **Star Wars** — massive popularity, multi-generational, fantasy-adjacent. The
   "no" case: existing TTRPGs from FFG/EDGE Studio, fans deeply attached to
   those systems, plus the genre fit (sci-fi space opera) doesn't map to D&D's
   medieval fantasy core. **Apples-to-apples in the sense that everyone's first
   "what about Star Wars" question gets addressed with data.**

C. **Final Fantasy** — JRPG fantasy, beloved, popular. The "no" case: fans
   deeply attached to Final Fantasy's specific job/class systems and ATB combat;
   conversion to D&D would feel like translation loss. Square Enix has its own
   licensing dynamics. **Apples-to-apples in the sense that "iconic fantasy
   game" is the surface read.**

My pick: **The Witcher**. The "IP has its own RPG ecosystem → high D&D
conversion CAC" insight is genuinely new and pedagogically rich. It's
apples-to-apples (both Hollow Knight and Witcher are video-game IPs with
passionate fanbases) and the "no" verdict comes from a structural insight,
not just "this IP doesn't fit." Star Wars has the same structural property
but the genre-mismatch dimension makes it slightly less clean.

If you'd rather, **Star Wars** is the more provocative choice for an executive
audience because it answers a question every exec asks at some point.
